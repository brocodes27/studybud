Faraday's Law of Induction

Faraday's law of induction (briefly, Faraday's law) is a basic law of electromagnetism predicting how a magnetic field will interact with an electric circuit to produce an electromotive force (EMF)—a phenomenon known as electromagnetic induction.

vibration

Diagram: Visualizing Magnetic Flux ($\Phi_B$) through a conducting loop moving in a uniform field.

It is the fundamental operating principle of transformers, inductors, and many types of electrical motors, generators and solenoids. The law states:

"The induced electromotive force in any closed circuit is equal to the negative of the time rate of change of the magnetic flux enclosed by the circuit."

Mathematically, for a single loop of wire, it is expressed as:
ε = - dΦB / dt

Where ε is the electromotive force and ΦB is the magnetic flux. The negative sign is a result of Lenz's Law, indicating that the direction of the induced EMF and resulting current will oppose the change in magnetic flux that created it.
